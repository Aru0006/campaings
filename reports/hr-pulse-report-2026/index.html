About this report

The state of Indian HR, measured — not guessed

This report summarises findings from the India HR Pulse 2026 survey, a community initiative by Zimyo Research to benchmark how HR and business leaders across India — and select GCC markets — are experiencing attrition, leadership, data maturity, and the changing shape of HR's mandate.

Methodology

How the pulse was taken

Sample size: 4,117 verified, anonymous responses collected between 1 June and 6 August 2026. Respondents span organisations of varying size, industry, and geography across India and select GCC markets.

This is a community-sourced pulse check rather than a statistically representative national survey. Figures should be read as directional signals from an engaged HR leadership community, not as population-level estimates.

Note on sample size: with 4,117 responses, individual percentages can shift meaningfully with just one or two additional responses. We call this out transparently rather than implying a larger base, and will update this report as the dataset grows.

Section 11

Work Model Mix: WFO / Hybrid / WFH

Remote work is no longer a debate — it's a default consideration for every organisation post-COVID. Here's what our benchmark reveals about where companies have landed.

Zimyo Concludes

  • Hybrid is the dominant working model reported by Indian organisations, at 45%–60% — the single largest category by a clear margin.
  • Complete WFO remains substantial at 35%–50%, indicating a significant share of organisations have not adopted any form of remote flexibility.
  • Complete WFH remains the least common model, at just 5%–10% — fully remote organisations are still the exception rather than the norm.
  • Taken together, over 80%–90% of organisations report operating on-site or hybrid, confirming that some in-office presence remains the default expectation across most of India's corporate landscape.
✦ Zimfact

Around 34% of Indian organisations now provide a dedicated one-time home-office setup allowance, reflecting the growing shift towards supporting productive hybrid and remote work environments.

Zimyo Concludes

  • Nearly 7 in 10 organisations (69.1%) make HR decisions without any structured employee data at all — primarily a habit and activation gap, not a retention problem.
  • Reliance on data rises sharply with company size: from 25.8% in Under-100 organisations to 73.3% in Enterprise (1000+).
  • Enterprise organisations that do use data run a small but meaningful quarterly cadence alongside a dominant monthly practice, versus smaller organisations that lean on an annual, one-off check.
  • Even among organisations that start using data, more than half (53.5%) never return after their first attempt.

Section 15

HR Team Size Ratio

HR staffing ratio moves in the opposite direction to every other parameter in this report: it falls as company size increases, from 3–4 HR staff per 100 employees (Under 100) down to just 1.5–2 (Enterprise 1000+). It's a useful lens for benchmarking: an organisation whose HR ratio sits meaningfully outside its segment's range may be under- or over-staffed relative to peers of similar size.

Zimyo Concludes

  • A roughly 2x difference between the smallest and largest organisations — smaller organisations carry a proportionally heavier HR overhead per employee.
  • The decline is steady across every segment step, reflecting classic economies of scale.
  • For smaller organisations, HR staff are likely generalists handling a broad range of functions, while larger organisations can afford more specialised HR roles.

Section 17

New Labour Code Readiness

Readiness for the new Labour Codes climbs steadily with company size, but exposure remains widespread even at the top end.

Zimyo Concludes

  • Half of all Under-100 organisations (169 of 337, 50%) are not ready for the new Labour Codes — the single largest exposure pocket in this report, in absolute organisation count.
  • Readiness rises steadily with company size, from 25% (Under 100) to 55% (Enterprise 1,000+) — but even at the top end, nearly half of Enterprise organisations (45%) are only partially ready or not ready at all.
  • Enterprise organisations are more than 2x as likely to be fully ready as Under-100 organisations (55% vs. 25%).
  • Combining "Partially Ready" and "Not Yet Ready": 75% of Under-100, 68% of 100–250, 58% of 250–1,000, and 45% of Enterprise organisations have not fully completed their transition.
✦ Zimfact

Size doesn't guarantee compliance. Even among Enterprise organisations, 45% are still not fully ready for the new Labour Codes.

Where To Go From Here

The India HR Pulse 2026 findings point to a consistent story: HR leaders know where the gaps are — in data, in retention, in compliance — and the challenge is closing them at scale. A few suggested next steps:

Move from one-off surveys to continuous listening

69.1% of organisations still decide without structured employee data. Standing up always-on pulse surveys and dashboards, rather than annual check-ins, is the fastest way to close this gap.

Get ahead of Labour Code compliance now

With 46.6% of organisations not yet ready, a structured readiness audit today avoids scrambling once enforcement timelines tighten.

Treat attrition as a tracked metric, not a feeling

Nearly 3 in 10 organisations don't track attrition precisely at all. Consistent, benchmarked tracking is the first step to fixing retention, not just discussing it.

Benchmark before you budget

HR team ratios, offer-to-join rates, and benefit adoption all vary sharply by company size. Knowing where your organisation sits against size-matched peers sharpens every HR budget conversation.
