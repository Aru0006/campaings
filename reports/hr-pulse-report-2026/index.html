About this report

The state of Indian HR, measured — not guessed

This report summarises findings from the India HR Pulse 2026 survey, a community initiative by Zimyo Research to benchmark how HR and business leaders across India — and select GCC markets — are experiencing attrition, leadership, data maturity, and the changing shape of HR's mandate.

The picture that emerges is a profession under pressure to modernise: retention is the number-one worry, most decisions are still made without structured data, and nearly half of organisations aren't yet ready for the new Labour Codes. The full breakdown — with sector cuts and the levers that move each metric — is below.

Methodology

How the pulse was taken

Sample size: 4,117 verified, anonymous responses collected between 1 June and 6 August 2026. Respondents span organisations of varying size, industry, and geography across India and select GCC markets.

Figures are reported as medians or shares of respondents unless stated otherwise; sector and size cuts require a minimum sample of 30 organisations. No individual person or company is identifiable — data is published only in aggregate.

01 · Attrition & retention

Retention is HR's number-one challenge — by a distance

59.3% of leaders named attrition and retention their single biggest challenge for the year — ahead of hiring, cost, and compliance. The pain is sharpest in organisations without a clear growth path for employees.

02 · The data gap

Most HR decisions are still made on gut, not data

69.1% of leaders admit they make key HR decisions without structured employee data. The organisations that have closed this gap report materially lower attrition and faster hiring.

69.1%

decide without structured data

Only 30.9% say their people decisions are consistently backed by data they trust. Among the data-mature minority, attrition runs ~7 points lower and offer-to-join is ~9 points higher — the clearest ROI signal in this year's survey.

03 · Hiring & offer-to-join

Offers convert well — but the drop-off is sector-specific

The overall offer-to-join rate across organisations analysed was 78.4%. Technology and BFSI convert best; manufacturing and retail lose the most candidates between offer and joining.

Nearly half aren't Labour Code ready
